Only 2 pairs exceed 30% and none exceed 35%. There is no duplicate or templated prose in the indexable set, and zero duplicate titles or meta descriptions.

Responsive — PASS.

90 measurements (18 routes × 5 viewports from 320px to 1920px) produced zero horizontal-scroll or overflow failures, zero console errors, and a cumulative layout shift of 0 on every page tested.

Design — GOOD, with one real accessibility defect.

Layout, spacing, type scale, and component consistency are solid and the palette is disciplined. But the orange homepage hero fails WCAG AA colour contrast for its small text: the lead paragraph reads 2.61:1 and the “FREE UNICODE FONTS” eyebrow 2.38:1, against a 4.5:1 requirement. This is the single highest-value design fix on the site. Details in section 6.

SEO hygiene — two content chores outstanding.

public/llms.txt lists only 17 URLs out of 61 indexable pages, and 26 titles exceed 60 characters so Google will truncate them in results. Neither blocks indexing. See section 9.

Why it holds up

  • The shell is fluid by default: width: min(1120px, calc(100% - 2rem)), so there is no fixed pixel width to overflow.
  • Grids use repeat(auto-fill, minmax(...)) and minmax(0, 1fr) rather than fixed columns, so tracks shrink instead of forcing a scrollbar.
  • Toolbars and chip rows are flex-wrap: wrap, which is why the LinkedIn formatter's 11-button toolbar reflows to two rows at 390px instead of clipping.
  • Long unbroken strings are handled with overflow-wrap: anywhere / word-break in 20 places — essential when users paste Unicode with no spaces.
  • Navigation switches to a hamburger below 720px; the desktop nav is hidden rather than squeezed.
  • Headings use clamp() so type scales with the viewport instead of stepping at breakpoints.

Breakpoints in use: 520px, 640px, 720px, 800px, 900px, plus a prefers-reduced-motion block that disables the entrance animations.

6. Is the design OK?

Mostly yes. The visual system is coherent and the implementation is careful. One genuine accessibility defect and a few smaller items are worth your time.

What is working

  • Disciplined palette. 391 CSS custom properties driving a white canvas, charcoal ink, and orange reserved for action. Body text is 15.1:1 contrast; secondary text 6.7:1. Both comfortably clear AA.
  • Zero layout shift. CLS is exactly 0 on all 8 pages measured, which is unusual and worth protecting.
  • Consistent components. Chips, copy buttons, tool stages, and preview panels are reused across every tool, so the new LinkedIn pages look native rather than bolted on.
  • Accessibility basics are in place. A working skip link, one <h1> per page, correct header/nav/main/footer landmarks, lang="en", a visible 2px focus ring on every control, and zero unnamed buttons or links and zero unlabelled form inputs across the 12 pages audited.
  • No images to go wrong. The site renders zero <img> elements — everything is text and Unicode — so there is no missing-alt-text debt and nothing to lazy-load.

The one real defect: homepage hero contrast

Small text on the orange hero fails WCAG AA.

The hero background is #ff5e1f. Pure white on that is only 3.06:1, and the hero's text is semi-transparent white, which makes it worse. The <h1> itself is fine (large text only needs 3:1), but everything smaller in the hero fails.

Two ways to fix it, in order of how much they disturb the brand:

  • Least disruptive: keep #ff5e1f as the hero background but make the lead text fully opaque white and raise it to 18.66px bold, which qualifies as large text at the 3:1 threshold. The eyebrow and the specimen chips would still need darkening.
  • Cleanest: darken the hero background to your existing --accent-deep (#c2410c). White text on that measures 5.18:1 and every element in the hero passes at any size, with no markup changes. It does make the hero noticeably deeper orange, so it is your call as a brand decision — I did not change it unilaterally.

Smaller design notes

  • Very small label text. Seven labels render between 10.9px and 11.8px (the support badges “Works everywhere”, “Mixed support”, and the eyebrow text). They pass contrast on white, but 11px is below what is comfortable on a phone. Consider a 12px floor.
  • Footer and nav link targets. Vertical link lists are about 21px tall. Inline links in prose are exempt from the 24px target-size guideline, but these block-level list links technically are not. Adding padding-block: .3rem to footer column links would resolve it.
  • Input and panel borders. --line (#e5e5e5) is 1.26:1 against white and --line-strong (#d1d1d1) is 1.53:1. WCAG 1.4.11 asks for 3:1 on the visible boundary of a control. Panel dividers are fine as-is; the borders on textareas and buttons are the ones worth darkening, since that boundary is the only thing marking them as interactive.

8. Fixed while auditing

Three defects surfaced during measurement and are already fixed and committed:

  • Heading-level skips on the three new pages. /linkedin-text-formatter/, /chatgpt-to-linkedin/, and /unformat-text/ jumped from <h1> straight to <h3>, which breaks screen-reader document navigation and muddies the outline Google reads. Promoted to <h2> (and the nested “Section dividers” heading from <h4> to <h3>). All 12 audited pages now report zero heading skips. Appearance is unchanged because those headings carry explicit font sizing.
  • Active nav link contrast. The current-page nav pill used --accent on --accent-soft — 2.82:1, below AA. Switched to --accent-deep for 4.79:1.
  • Checkbox touch targets. The option checkboxes on the AI and unformat tools rendered at the browser default 13×13px. Bumped to 1.15rem. Their labels were already clickable, so this is comfort rather than a functional fix.

Verified after the changes: production build compiles with all 151 routes prerendered, sitemap.xml still at 61 URLs, ESLint clean, check:unicode clean across 67 styles, and check:text clean across 16 exact round trips, 3 case-insensitive round trips, 8 preserved scripts, and both Markdown modes.

9. Recommended next actions

In the order I would do them.

  1. Decide on the hero contrast fix. The only genuine accessibility failure left, and it is on your highest-traffic page. Needs a brand call from you (section 6).
  2. Regenerate public/llms.txt from the page registry at build time. It currently lists 17 URLs against 61 indexable pages, so AI crawlers see about a quarter of the site. Wiring it into the same prebuild step that already writes sitemap.xml means it can never drift again — this is a small, self-contained change.
  3. Shorten 26 over-long titles. Google truncates around 60 characters. The worst offenders are /guides/linkedin-formatting-not-working/ at 77 and /cool-text-generator/ at 73. The  | FancifyText suffix is what pushes most of them over; dropping it on the longest titles is the cheapest win.
  4. Trim 8 meta descriptions over 160 characters, including the three new pages (/linkedin-text-formatter/ is 214).
  5. Deepen the thinnest indexable pages listed in section 4, starting with /glitch-text-generator/ at 288 words.
  6. Raise the small-label floor to 12px and darken control borders to clear 3:1 (section 6).

Beyond fixes, the roadmap gaps identified in the earlier competitive research are still open: a standalone /ai-text-cleaner/ page, commodity utilities (word counter, character counter, text repeater, remove line breaks), a standalone LinkedIn symbol library, an /answers/ Q&A surface, “X vs Y” and *-alternative comparison pages, a /how-we-test/ trust page, and an alt-codes plus Unicode-inspector section.

10. How this was measured

  • Page counts and keywords were read from src/data/pages/registry.ts and src/lib/seo/sitemap-xml.ts by bundling the real TypeScript with esbuild and calling the same functions the sitemap generator uses — so these are the shipping values, not a hand transcription.
  • Uniqueness compared the <main> element of every built HTML file. Text was lowercased and reduced to ASCII words of 2+ characters (this deliberately discards the Unicode glyph grids, which would otherwise make every style page look similar), then split into overlapping 5-word shingles and compared with Jaccard similarity across all 1,830 indexable page pairs.
  • Responsiveness, targets, and contrast were measured in headless Chrome against the production server, reading computed styles and bounding boxes from the live DOM at each viewport.
  • Contrast caveat: the automated walker resolves background-color only, so elements sitting on the dark gradient preview panels initially reported false failures. Those were verified against the CSS (#1a1a1a to #111111, roughly 16:1 with near-white text) and excluded. Only genuine failures are listed in section 6.
  • Not covered: no real-device or field testing, no throttled Lighthouse run, and no manual screen-reader pass. The timings in section 7 come from a local server with no network throttling and should not be read as field Core Web Vitals.
